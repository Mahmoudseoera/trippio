The 8 Best Places to Visit in Luxor

Discover the top attractions in Luxor — from breathtaking river cruises and mountain viewpoints to ancient temples and royal tombs.

Sara Ahmed Travel Writer, Brand Name
Updated Jun 15, 2026 7 min read
Share

Often called the world's greatest open-air museum, Luxor is where ancient Egypt feels closest. Set on the banks of the Nile roughly 400 miles south of Cairo, this once-capital city holds more royal tombs, temples, and monuments per square mile than almost anywhere on earth.

There's plenty to see here, and with a smart plan you can cover the essentials in two to three days without rushing. This guide walks you through the sights that give the best return on your time — grouped by what kind of traveler you are, so you can build a route that fits.

Luxor's top places to visit at a glance

  • Best places in Luxor for history: Karnak Temple Complex, the Valley of the Kings
  • Great places in Luxor for a Nile experience: Felucca sail at sunset and Banana Island boat tour
  • Best places in Luxor for views: Hot air balloon ride, Hatshepsut's Temple

Best places in Luxor for history

1. Karnak Temple Complex

You'll find the Karnak Temple Complex just outside central Luxor, and it's easily the largest religious site ever built. Give it at least two hours — the Great Hypostyle Hall alone, with its 134 towering columns, is worth lingering in as the light changes through the afternoon.

Highlight: Finding a quiet spot near the sacred lake as tour groups thin out late afternoon — it's one of the few places in Luxor you can sit undisturbed among 3,000-year-old stone.

East Bank, Luxor Daily, 6:00 AM – 5:30 PM From $25

2. Valley of the Kings

On the West Bank, cut into the desert cliffs, lie more than 60 tombs built for pharaohs of the New Kingdom — Tutankhamun among them. Standard entry gets you into three tombs; if the painted walls of Tutankhamun's chamber or Seti I's tomb interest you, budget for the separate add-on tickets.

Highlight: The colour is what stays with people — 3,000 years underground and the ceiling paintings in Ramses VI's tomb still look freshly done.

West Bank, Luxor Daily, 6:00 AM – 5:00 PM From $30

Best places in Luxor for a Nile experience

3. Sunset felucca sail

After a morning of temples and tombs, a slow sail on a traditional wooden felucca is the reset Luxor asks for. There's no engine, no itinerary — just the sound of water and the Theban hills turning orange as the sun drops behind them.

Highlight: Book the last sailing of the day — boats thin out and you'll often have a stretch of river to yourselves for the actual sunset.

Luxor Corniche Daily, from 4:00 PM From $15

Best places in Luxor for views

4. Hot air balloon over the Valley of the Kings

Pickup is around 4:30 AM, which puts off a lot of travelers — don't let it put you off. Drifting over the West Bank as the sun comes up, with the temples and tombs laid out below in the early light, is consistently rated Luxor's best single experience.

Highlight: Flights are weather-dependent and sell out days ahead in high season — book before you land in Luxor, not once you're there.

West Bank launch site Daily, sunrise departure From $95

5. Hatshepsut's Temple

Built for Egypt's most powerful female pharaoh and set directly into a limestone cliff face, this terraced temple looks almost modern next to Luxor's other monuments. Go early — there's no shade, and the reflected heat off the stone terraces climbs fast after 10 AM.

Best places in Luxor for culture and history

6. Luxor Museum

Smaller and far less crowded than Cairo's Egyptian Museum, Luxor Museum focuses on quality over quantity — well-lit, well-labeled pieces from Karnak and the royal tombs, including two royal mummies. An hour here adds real context to everything you'll see on the West Bank.

7. Colossi of Memnon

Two 60-foot stone statues, all that remains of a temple that no longer exists, standing alone in a field beside the road to the West Bank sites. It costs nothing and takes ten minutes — worth a stop on your way to or from the Valley of the Kings.

8. Luxor Temple at night

Unlike most of Luxor's sites, this one is best seen after dark. Floodlit columns, an almost-empty courtyard, and none of the daytime heat — a relaxed way to close out a day of temple-hopping, and an easy walk from most East Bank hotels.
